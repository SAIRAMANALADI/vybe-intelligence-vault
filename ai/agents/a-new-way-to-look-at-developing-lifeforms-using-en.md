# A new way to look at developing lifeforms, using energy to predict action - Princeton Engineering

## Summary

- **ATP spatial mapping predicts tissue folding**: Researchers at Princeton University demonstrated that localized ATP concentration gradients in developing tissues (e.g., mouse eyes, chicken lungs, fruit fly embryos) correlate with mechanical folding events, enabling *in silico* prediction of morphogenetic shape changes.

- **Mitochondrial clustering as a mechanical precursor**: Prior to tissue folding, mitochondria aggregate asymmetrically along specific tissue boundaries, releasing ATP to power actomyosin-driven contractility, establishing a causal link between cellular metabolism and biomechanical force generation.

- **Metabolic-spatial omics paradigm shift**: The study introduces a framework integrating *spatial metabolomics* (ATP distribution) with *spatial transcriptomics*, challenging traditional developmental models by positioning cellular energy dynamics as a primary driver of organogenesis.
