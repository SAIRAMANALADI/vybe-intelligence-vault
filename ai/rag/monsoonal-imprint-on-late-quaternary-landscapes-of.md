# Monsoonal imprint on late Quaternary landscapes of the Rub’ al Khali Desert | Communications Earth & Environment

## Summary

- **Holocene Humid Period evidence**: Geologic reconstructions and Earth system model simulations reveal a ~1100 km² lake formation in the Rub’ al Khali Desert, breached by a ~150 km-long valley due to intense seasonal precipitation and repeated heavy monsoonal events, challenging prior assumptions of gradual landscape stabilization.

- **Monsoonal-driven sediment transport**: Lacustrine and fluvial deposits indicate sediment routing up to 1000 km from the Asir Mountains, demonstrating extensive hydrological connectivity and rapid landscape transformation under enhanced African Monsoon conditions.

- **Human-climate interaction**: The study links monsoonal-driven humid phases to the formation of grasslands/savannahs, facilitating significant human expansions across Arabia during the early to middle Holocene, with local variability in humid period duration.
