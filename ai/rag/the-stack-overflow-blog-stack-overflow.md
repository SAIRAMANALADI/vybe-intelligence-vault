## Summary

- **Acoustic Propagation in Atmosphere**: Sound from meteor entry (e.g., sonic booms, hypersonic shockwaves) attenuates rapidly in the rarefied upper atmosphere (~100 km altitude), making it inaudible at ground level under typical conditions.

- **Energy Dissipation Mechanisms**: Hypersonic meteor entry (>12 km/s) generates plasma and intense thermal radiation; acoustic energy is predominantly dissipated as heat or radiated into space, further reducing ground-level sound intensity.

- **Observational Evidence**: Historical accounts and infrasound monitoring confirm that only exceptionally large bolides (e.g., Chelyabinsk, 2013) produce detectable ground-level sounds, supporting Sagan’s assertion for typical meteors.
