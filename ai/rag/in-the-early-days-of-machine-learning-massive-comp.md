# In the Early Days of Machine Learning, Massive Computers Said George Harrison Was a Woman. A.I. Has Come a Long Way

## Summary

- **Mark I Perceptron (1958)**: Cornell's Frank Rosenblatt developed a room-sized neural network of 400 sensors mimicking a single neuron, using feedback-based learning to classify images (e.g., misclassifying George Harrison as a woman due to hairstyle), pioneering early AI via trial-and-error weight adjustments.

- **Neural Network Revival (1980s–2000s)**: Post-Perceptron stagnation ended with Yann LeCun’s Bell Labs ZIP code recognition system (1989), leveraging handwriting examples; advancements accelerated via GPUs (parallel processing) and internet-scale data, enabling modern deep learning.

- **AI Evolution Drivers**: GPUs enabled massively parallel computations, while internet-scale datasets (text, images, video) allowed AI to surpass early limitations, transitioning from rule-based logic to data-driven neural networks capable of complex perceptual tasks.
