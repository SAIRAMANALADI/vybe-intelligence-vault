# Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, 510 U.S. 569 (1994).

## Summary

- **Fair Use Doctrine Application**: The Supreme Court ruled that 2 Live Crew's commercial parody of Roy Orbison's "Oh, Pretty Woman" qualified as fair use under 17 U.S.C. § 107, emphasizing the transformative nature of parody and its social benefit in critiquing the original work, despite its commercial intent.

- **Four-Factor Analysis**: The Court applied the statutory fair use factors: (1) the commercial purpose weighed against fair use but was outweighed by the transformative purpose; (2) the nature of the copyrighted work (creative musical composition) favored Acuff-Rose; (3) the amount used was limited to what was necessary to conjure the original; and (4) no market harm to the original was demonstrated.

- **Transformative Use Standard**: The Court established that transformative works (e.g., parodies) are central to fair use, as they further copyright's goal of promoting creativity, with commerciality being less significant when the use is highly transformative.
