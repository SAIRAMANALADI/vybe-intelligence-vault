# differential geometry - Total curvature and Stokes' theorem on the hemisphere - Mathematics Stack Exchange

## Summary

- The connection form $\omega_{12} = \sin v \, du$ derived from the spherical parametrization is **not globally defined** on the upper hemisphere due to a singularity at the north pole, violating the requirements for applying Stokes' theorem.

- The Gaussian curvature integral $\int_H K \, dM = 2\pi$ over the hemisphere cannot be equated to $-\int_\alpha \omega_{12}$ because the latter evaluates to zero (as $\alpha^* \omega_{12} = 0$ along the equator), highlighting the failure of Stokes' theorem in this context due to the non-exactness of $K \, dA$ globally.

- A valid computation requires a **globally defined orthonormal frame** (e.g., via stereographic projection) or a limiting argument where the singularity is excluded, recovering the missing $2\pi$ contribution from a small latitude circle near the pole.
