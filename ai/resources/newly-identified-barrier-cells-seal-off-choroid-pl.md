# Newly identified barrier cells seal off choroid plexus from CSF, rest of brain | The Transmitter: Neuroscience News and

## Summary

- **Novel Fibroblast-Derived Barrier**: A previously unrecognized population of CLDN11-expressing fibroblasts at the base of the choroid plexus forms a sealed barrier via adherens and tight junctions, distinct from the blood-brain barrier and blood-CSF barrier, confirmed in mice and human postmortem samples.

- **Functional Compartmentalization**: The fibroblast-derived "base barrier" restricts tracer diffusion between blood and CSF, becoming selectively leaky during inflammation (e.g., lipopolysaccharide-induced), potentially serving as a preferential entry route for immune cells into the brain.

- **Developmental and Evolutionary Conservation**: Lineage tracing reveals meningeal origin of these fibroblasts, highlighting interconnected brain borders early in development; CLDN11+ cells with tight junction patterns are conserved in humans, suggesting a species-wide protective mechanism.
