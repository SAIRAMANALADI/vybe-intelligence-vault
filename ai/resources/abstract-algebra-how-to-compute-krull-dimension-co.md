# abstract algebra - How to compute Krull dimension concretely - Mathematics Stack Exchange

## Summary

- **For \( R = \mathbb{C}[x, y]/(x^{100}, y^{100}) \):** The ideal \((x^{100}, y^{100})\) is nilpotent, forcing all prime ideals to contain \((x, y)\). The only prime ideal is \((x, y)/(x^{100}, y^{100})\), yielding \(\dim R = 0\).

- **For \( R = \mathbb{Z}_p/(p^{10}) \):** The ring is isomorphic to \(\mathbb{Z}/p^{10}\mathbb{Z}\), an Artinian local ring with a unique prime ideal \((p)/(p^{10})\), so \(\dim R = 0\).

- **General principle:** Artinian rings (with nilpotent ideals) always have Krull dimension zero, as their prime spectra consist of a single maximal ideal.
