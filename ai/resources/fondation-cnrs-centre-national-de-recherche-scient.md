# Fondation CNRS - Centre National de Recherche Scientifique

## Summary

- **Limited U.S. Involvement at Verdun**: Despite the U.S. entering WWI in April 1917, American forces played a minimal role in the Battle of Verdun (1916), with only a small contingent of U.S. military observers and volunteers attached to French units.

- **Memorialization Efforts**: The Mémorial de Verdun features exhibits honoring three specific Americans—Col. William J. "Wild Bill" Donovan, Capt. Charles E. Stanton, and volunteer nurse Helen Fairchild—whose contributions to WWI are highlighted despite their limited direct involvement in Verdun.

- **Historical Narrative Correction**: The article emphasizes correcting the U.S. WWI memory by acknowledging early American contributions (e.g., Stanton’s "Lafayette, we are here!" quote) and the role of volunteers, countering the dominant focus on the 1917 U.S. entry.
