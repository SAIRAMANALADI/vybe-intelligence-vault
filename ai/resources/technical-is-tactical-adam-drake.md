# Technical is tactical - Adam Drake

## Summary

- **Hierarchical Problem-Solving**: Military doctrine (FM 3-0) defines three interdependent levels of problem-solving: strategic (policy/national interests), operational (campaigns/major operations), and tactical (short-term actions/battles). Business parallels exist, where operational success depends on people/process optimization, while tactical fixes (e.g., tooling) often address symptoms rather than root causes.

- **Costs of Tactical Fixes**: Band-aid technical solutions incur hidden long-term costs (training, maintenance, support) and rarely address systemic issues rooted in organizational dysfunction (e.g., poor leadership, inadequate processes). Firefighting cultures emerge when tactical solutions are prioritized over strategic improvements.

- **Root Cause Analysis**: Frameworks like *5 Whys* or breaking down pain points into constituent parts reveal systemic issues (e.g., lack of training, onboarding failures). Tactical technical fixes provide temporary relief but fail to resolve underlying problems, which are typically organizational (e.g., ineffective leadership, process gaps).
