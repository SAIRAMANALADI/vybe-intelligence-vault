# Copyright 101 - Copyright - LibGuides at Cornell University

## Summary

- **Automatic Copyright in the U.S.**: Copyright protection is granted automatically upon creation of a work in a fixed medium (e.g., written text, recorded audio), without requiring registration or a copyright notice; creators retain six exclusive rights: reproduction, derivative works, distribution, public performance/display, and digital audio transmission (for sound recordings).

- **Exclusions and Limitations**: Copyright does not protect facts, ideas, or basic elements (e.g., recipes, mathematical formulas, alphabets); limitations include **Fair Use** (Section 107) and library/archival reproductions (Sections 108–112), which allow specific uses without permission under defined conditions.

- **IP Distinctions**: Copyright protects creative expression in fixed form, while **trademarks** (brand identifiers like logos) and **patents** (mechanical/chemical processes) require federal registration and serve distinct purposes (branding vs. exclusive production rights).
