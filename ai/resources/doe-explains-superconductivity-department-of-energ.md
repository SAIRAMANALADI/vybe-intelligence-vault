# DOE Explains...Superconductivity | Department of Energy

## Summary

- **Mechanism of Superconductivity**: Superconductivity arises when electrons form Cooper pairs below a critical temperature (Tc), bound by phonon-mediated interactions, enabling resistance-free current flow as per the BCS theory (Nobel Prize 1972).

- **High-Temperature Superconductors (HTS)**: Discovered in 1986, copper-oxide and iron-based materials exhibit superconductivity at elevated temperatures (e.g., above liquid nitrogen), defying conventional BCS theory and prompting new quantum explanations.

- **Applications & Challenges**: Superconductors enable high-field magnets (e.g., MRI, synchrotrons) but face critical current limits; DOE-funded research targets enhancing Tc, current density, and discovering new materials via theoretical/experimental studies.
