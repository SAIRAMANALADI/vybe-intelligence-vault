# Ninety–ninety rule - Wikipedia

## Summary

- **Humorous aphorism** in software engineering stating that the first 90% of code takes 90% of time, while the remaining 10% of code takes another 90% of time, totaling 180% effort.

- **Attributed to Tom Cargill** (Bell Labs) and popularized by Jon Bentley in a 1985 *Communications of the ACM* column, highlighting the unpredictability of software development timelines.

- **Common in Agile projects** where tasks marked "relatively done" often stall due to unresolved final dependencies, reflecting the rule's real-world applicability in project management.
